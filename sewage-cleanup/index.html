Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Across La Habra, La Habra Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.
